The prime lattice — (3, 5) plane

Each axis is a prime. Each point is 3a·5b, folded into one octave by powers of 2.

Reading the lattice. Step right = multiply by 3 (and divide by 2 to stay in the octave) → up a perfect fifth. Step up = multiply by 5 (and divide by 4) → up a pure major third. Step diagonally = combinations.

The shaded triangle is a major triad: root + M3 + P5 (1/1, 5/4, 3/2). One step up and one step right of the root. Every major triad anywhere on the lattice is this same L-shape; every minor triad is its mirror.

Distance ≈ dissonance. Notes near the origin share many partials with the root (small integers in the ratio). Notes far away — large integers — sound increasingly remote. The "Tenney distance" of a ratio p/q is roughly log(p·q).

The third axis. Adding prime 7 (the harmonic seventh, 7/4 ≈ 969 ¢) gives a third dimension. Adding 11, 13, … keeps adding axes. The full lattice is infinite-dimensional, one axis per prime.
